# Koc & Savas (2024): Voice-based AI Chatbots in English Language Learning
## Metadata
- Type: systematic meta-synthesis
- Date: 2026-07-07
- Source: ReCALL 37(1), 4-21, Cambridge. CC-BY.
- Authors: Fatma Seyma Koc, Perihan Savas
- Reliability: high (PRISMA, top CALL journal)
- Tags: sla, chatbot, AI, conversational-agent, meta-synthesis

## Why It Matters
Most relevant recent review of AI chatbot use in language learning. Provides evidence on theoretical frameworks, linguistic/affective outcomes, pedagogical strategies. Key gap: almost no studies testing SLA theory with LLM agents.

## Key Claims
1. Voice chatbots show positive results for both linguistic and affective outcomes.
2. Theoretical frameworks often underdeveloped -- most studies are atheoretical.
3. Chatbots create non-judgmental environments reducing anxiety, promoting WTC.
4. Speaking practice dominant; multiple skills supported.
5. Gaps: limited long-term data, minimal SLA framework use, almost no LLM studies.
6. Pedagogical integration matters more than technology.

## Evidence / Details
- 57 studies (2010-2024), PRISMA framework
- Pre-LLM era: most used pattern matching or limited NLU
- Strengths: reduced anxiety, motivation, 24/7 availability
- Challenges: speech recognition errors, shallow feedback, limited conversational flexibility

## Implications for VibeLingo
1. Affective advantage: reduced anxiety with non-judgmental AI is strongest finding.
2. Theoretical gap = opportunity: VibeLingo could contribute original SLA-with-LLM research.
3. LLM era changes everything: modern LLMs qualitatively different from reviewed chatbots.
4. Task embedding strength: VibeLingo embeds in real workflows, matching pedagogical recommendation.
5. Text modality: review focuses on voice; CMC research supports text, but LLM-mediated is unstudied.

## Limitations
- Voice-focused, not text-based LLM.
- Pre-LLM era tech.
- Short-term studies only.
- Publication bias likely.
