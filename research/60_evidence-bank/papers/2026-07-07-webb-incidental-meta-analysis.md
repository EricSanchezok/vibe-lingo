# How Effective Is Second Language Incidental Vocabulary Learning? A Meta-Analysis

## Metadata

- Type: paper
- Date captured: 2026-07-07
- Source URL: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507 (Language Teaching, Cambridge University Press)
- Authors: Stuart Webb, Takumi Uchihara, Akifumi Yanagisawa
- Year: 2023
- Reliability: high (comprehensive meta-analysis, 24 primary studies, 2,771 participants)
- Tags: sla, incidental-learning, vocabulary-acquisition, meta-analysis, reading, listening

## Why It Matters

This is the most comprehensive meta-analysis of incidental L2 vocabulary learning to date. It establishes baseline rates of incidental vocabulary acquisition (9-18% of target words learned from a single exposure session) and identifies key moderator variables. Essential for setting realistic expectations about how much vocabulary learning can occur during agent sessions.

## Key Claims

- Mean proportion of target words learned: **9-18% on immediate posttests, 6-17% on delayed posttests**.
- Learning is similar across reading (17%/15%), listening (15%/13%), and reading while listening (13%/17%).
- Viewing results in lower learning rates (7%/5%).
- **Spaced conditions produce larger effects** (g=1.51) than massed (g=0.97), approaching significance (p=.080).
- **More proficient learners learn more incidentally** (g=1.40 vs. 0.70 for basic learners).
- **Narrative texts produce more learning** (g=1.43) than expository texts (g=0.61).
- **Texts designed for L2 learners produce more learning** (g=1.56) than L1 texts (g=0.71).
- Form recognition decays sharply (18% → 6%); meaning recall is more durable (9% → 12%).

## Evidence / Details

- 24 primary studies, 29 effect sizes, N=2,771.
- Random-effects model; Hedges' g.
- Moderator variables: proficiency, institutional level, text type, text audience, spacing, mode of input, pre-knowledge control, test format.
- Learning rate calculated as (experimental gain - control gain) / total target words.
- Meaning recall is the most durable form of knowledge — it improves slightly at delayed posttest.

## Implications for VibeLingo

- **Realistic expectations**: In a single agent session, expect 9-18% of exposed words to be learned. This means repeated exposure across multiple sessions is essential.
- **Spacing multiplies effect**: The trend toward larger effects in spaced conditions supports distributing vocabulary exposure across sessions rather than bombarding in one session.
- **Text type matters for agent sessions**: Technical/expository agent responses may yield less incidental learning than narrative/descriptive responses.
- **Both input directions matter**: Reading (agent responses) and reading-while-listening are productive — VibeLingo should capture from both agent output and user input.
- **Form recognition is fragile**: Recognition decays fast without review — the SRS component must prevent this.
- **Meaning recall is durable but harder to acquire**: Focus more effort on meaning-recall review tasks.

## Limitations

- Most studies used single-session exposures with surprise tests — not continuous, embedded learning.
- Primarily university-level participants.
- Weighted toward English as L2.
- Does not address vocabulary capture: selection of which words to expose.

## Follow-up Questions

- What learning rates are achievable from technical/agent-specific discourse (not general reading)?
- How does the 9-18% per-session rate compound across multiple agent sessions over weeks?
- What is the optimal exposure frequency for agent-session vocabulary?
