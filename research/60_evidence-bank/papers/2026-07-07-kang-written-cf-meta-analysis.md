# A Meta-Analysis of Short- and Long-Term Effects of Written Corrective Feedback on L2 Grammatical Accuracy in Writing

- Authors: EunYoung Kang (Kongju National University)

## Why It Matters

While Lyster & Ranta (1997) and Lyster & Saito (2010) focus on oral classroom feedback, this meta-analysis focuses specifically on WRITTEN corrective feedback — which is the modality VibeLingo will use. Chat-based agent interaction is text, not speech. The finding that WCF effects decay from moderate (g=0.62) to small-moderate (g=0.46) on delayed posttests underscores the need for sustained, repeated feedback — not one-shot corrections.

## Key Claims

- Written corrective feedback (WCF) yields a **moderate overall effect (g=0.62)** on immediate posttests, decaying to **small-moderate (g=0.46)** on delayed posttests.
- Effect sizes are smaller than oral CF meta-analyses, suggesting writing-specific challenges.
- **Writing genre** was a significant moderator: narrative/expository writing showed different WCF effectiveness.
- **Feedback type** (direct vs. indirect) was a significant moderator on both immediate and delayed tests.
- **Focused feedback** (targeting specific error types) tends to be more effective than unfocused (correcting everything).

## Evidence / Details

- 25 primary studies with both immediate and delayed posttest designs.
- Overall WCF effect: g=0.62 immediate → g=0.46 delayed (medium → small-medium).
- Moderator analysis: genre of writing task and type of feedback were significant factors.
- Direct feedback (providing the correct form): more effective on immediate posttests.
- Indirect feedback (marking errors without correction): may promote deeper processing and better long-term retention, though evidence is mixed.
- Metalinguistic feedback (explanation + correction): consistently strong across studies.

## Implications for VibeLingo

- **WCF effects are smaller than oral CF**: Expect modest per-correction learning. Cumulative, repeated feedback is essential.
- **Focused > unfocused**: VibeLingo should prioritize feedback on the most impactful errors (those that impede communication or recur frequently), not try to fix everything.
- **Metalinguistic feedback is a best-of-both**: It combines direct correction with explanation, producing strong effects on both immediate and delayed tests.
- **Direct correction alone decays**: The user may fix the error once but not retain the rule. Explanation + correction is essential for durable learning.
- **Genre sensitivity**: Agent-session text (technical prompts, code comments, explanations) is unlike typical academic writing — WCF effectiveness in this genre is unknown.
- **Delayed test decay supports spaced review**: The g=0.62 → 0.46 drop means review/re-exposure is necessary.

## Limitations

- Primarily EFL academic writing contexts; not chat-based or workplace communication.
- Most studies target specific grammatical features (articles, past tense) — not holistic expression polish.
- Does not address vocabulary-level feedback vs. grammar-level feedback separately.

## Follow-up Questions

- Does WCF effectiveness differ between chat-style short texts and essay-length writing?
- What is the optimal focus scope for agent-session feedback (1 error? 3 errors? all?)
- How does WCF interact with the user's primary task (coding) — does feedback during coding interfere more than during pure writing?
