# Schmidt Noticing Hypothesis (1990, 2001, 2010)
## Metadata
- Type: paper
- Date: 2026-07-07
- Source: Schmidt 1990 Applied Linguistics 11(2); Schmidt 2001 in Robinson (Cognition and SLA); Wikipedia
- Authors: Richard Schmidt
- Reliability: high (foundational, debated)
- Tags: sla, noticing-hypothesis, attention, awareness, focus-on-form

## Why It Matters
Theoretical backbone for VibeLingo show-me-why. Schmidt argues learners must consciously notice forms for acquisition. If true, VibeLingo must explicitly draw attention to language features. Addresses core tension: implicit exposure vs. explicit attention.

## Key Claims
1. 1990 strong claim: subliminal language learning impossible -- noticing necessary for input-to-intake.
2. Noticing = conscious registration of surface forms, distinct from attention and understanding.
3. Noticing the gap (Gass): learners must notice discrepancy between output and target.
4. Schmidt & Frota (1986): features appeared in output only after noticing in input (diary study).
5. 2001 weaker claim: more noticing = more learning (not strictly necessary).
6. 2010: noticing helpful but not required for all language aspects.

## Criticisms
- Truscott (1998): cognitive psychology basis unclear; hypothesis too vague.
- Altman (1990): half of Hebrew learning could NOT be traced to noticing.
- Tomlin & Villa (1994): detection can occur without awareness; awareness not essential.
- N. Ellis: noticing only for new/difficult features; implicit learning default.
- Krashen: most acquisition is subconscious; noticing = learning, not acquisition.

## The 2001 Retreat
Schmidt limited noticing to surface forms only (words, constructions) -- NOT abstract rules which cannot be perceived in input. Weakened to more noticing, more learning.

## Implications for VibeLingo
1. show-me-why is theoretically justified (but strong claim is contested).
2. Focus on specific instances, not abstract grammar rules.
3. Input enhancement (highlighting, bolding) = low-friction noticing.
4. Noticing the gap: juxtapose user output with correct form.
5. Timing: save explicit feedback for task boundaries.
6. User preference for show-me-why aligns with research.

## Limitations
- Weak claim more defensible than strong claim.
- Methodological challenges measuring noticing.
- Surface-form scope limits applicability.
