# Oral Feedback in Classroom SLA: A Meta-Analysis

## Metadata

- Type: paper
- Date captured: 2026-07-07
- Source URL: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234580927 (Studies in Second Language Acquisition)
- Authors: Roy Lyster, Kazuya Saito
- Year: 2010
- Reliability: high (major meta-analysis, highly cited, directly compares CF types with effect sizes)
- Tags: sla, corrective-feedback, meta-analysis, prompts-vs-recasts, effect-sizes

## Why It Matters

This is the definitive meta-analysis comparing the effectiveness of different oral corrective feedback types. It provides effect sizes for prompts vs. recasts and identifies moderating variables (age, context, treatment length). The finding that prompts (d=0.83) significantly outperform recasts (d=0.53) on delayed posttests is the strongest evidence for prioritizing prompt-based feedback in VibeLingo's polish feature. Also confirmed CF effects are durable — they persist (and sometimes increase) on delayed posttests.

## Key Claims

- Corrective feedback has **significant and durable effects** on L2 development.
- **Prompts produce larger effects (d=0.83) than recasts (d=0.53)**, and the difference is more pronounced on delayed posttests.
- The advantage of prompts over recasts is **especially strong for young learners** — they may not notice recasts as corrections at all.
- **Longer treatment duration** produces larger effects: 1-2 hour treatments (d=0.44) vs. 6+ hour treatments (d=1.03).
- Effects were larger in **foreign language contexts** (d=0.85) than second language contexts (d=0.54).
- CF effects were durable: delayed posttest effects (d=0.73) were similar to or larger than immediate posttest effects (d=0.62).

## Evidence / Details

- 15 classroom-based studies included.
- Overall CF effect: d=0.74 on immediate, d=0.73 on delayed — large effect.
- Prompt vs. recast: prompt d=0.83, recast d=0.53 on posttests. Gap widens at delayed test.
- Moderator effects:
  - Age: young learners benefit more from prompts; older learners process recasts better.
  - Context: FL > SL context.
  - Duration: longer = better; minimum effective treatment ~3 hours.
  - Outcome measure: constrained constructed response > metalinguistic judgment > free constructed response.
- The recast disadvantage is partly explained by **ambiguity**: learners often interpret recasts as meaning confirmation, not error correction.

## Implications for VibeLingo

- **Favor prompts over recasts**: The meta-analytic evidence is clear. For VibeLingo's polish feature, the default feedback format should be prompt-based (metalinguistic hint + invitation to self-correct) rather than simple reformulation (recast).
- **Durability of CF is encouraging**: Feedback given during agent sessions should have lasting effects, not just immediate fixes. This supports the "persistence" design goal.
- **Cumulative effect**: Treatment length matters. A plugin that provides feedback consistently across many sessions (not just once) will produce larger effects through accumulated exposure.
- **Text-based adaptation**: The meta-analysis is on oral feedback. Text-based written feedback may have different dynamics — the user can process metalinguistic information more thoroughly in text.
- **Young vs. adult learners**: The prompt > recast gap is larger for younger learners. For adult users (likely VibeLingo's demographic), the gap is smaller but still significant — recasts are still useful, just suboptimal.

## Limitations

- Oral classroom CF only; written CF is a different literature.
- 15 studies is a relatively small meta-analysis by modern standards.
- Does not address individual differences (working memory, aptitude, anxiety).
- Mostly English/French L2 contexts.

## Follow-up Questions

- Do the prompt > recast findings replicate in written, text-based CF contexts?
- How should VibeLingo sequence feedback: immediate prompt first, then recast if user can't self-correct?
- Does the agent's text interface change the "ambiguity of recasts" problem?
