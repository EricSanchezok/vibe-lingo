## Why It Matters
Comprehensive scoping review (43 studies) of how chatbots affect student motivation. Directly relevant to VibeLingo.

## Key Claims
- 31 of 43 studies used at least one theoretical model (SDT, ARCS, expectancy-value).
- Most studied motivational variables: engagement, interest, intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy.
- Chatbot interactions generally positively correlated with motivation, but chatbots lagged human feedback and pedagogical approaches.
- Only 4 studies were longitudinal. Most treated motivation as a static outcome.
- Recent chatbots feature diverse capabilities and generative AI for enhanced interactivity.
- Sustained interest was related to prior human interactions.

## Evidence / Details
- Scoping review of 43 empirical studies (1966-2023).
- 24 studies compared chatbots vs control groups: 12 positive, chatbots outperformed non-chatbot but underperformed human feedback.
- Interactive/constructive chatbot designs motivated better than passive ones.
- 5 studies found chatbot feedback groups had lower self-efficacy than human feedback groups.

## Implications for VibeLingo
- Chatbot interaction CAN support motivation, but chatbots alone are not a silver bullet.
- The finding that chatbots lag human feedback is less critical since VibeLingo embeds in real work, not isolated practice.
- Engagement and intrinsic motivation were key outcomes ¡ª aligns with VibeLingo autonomy-first approach.
- Need to measure motivation longitudinally, not just at end of studies.
- Interactive designs (constructive, not passive) are more motivating.

## Limitations
- Only 4 longitudinal studies in the entire review.
- Most studies measured short-term motivation.
- Limited to published studies through 2023, pre-dating most LLM-based chatbots.

## Follow-up Questions
- How do LLM-based chatbots compare to earlier rule-based systems for motivation?
- Does VibeLingo embedded-in-work context outperform standalone chatbot practice?
