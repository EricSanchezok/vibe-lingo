# Loewen & Sato (2018): Interaction and Instructed SLA
## Metadata
- Type: review
- Date: 2026-07-07
- Source: Language Teaching 51(3), 285-329
- Authors: Shawn Loewen, Masatoshi Sato
- Reliability: high
- Tags: sla, interaction-hypothesis, meta-analysis, CMC

## Why It Matters
Most important review for VibeLingo. Surveys 30+ years of interaction research. Includes computer-mediated interaction section -- directly relevant to text-based human-LLM interaction.

## Key Claims
1. Multiple meta-analyses converge on large interaction effects.
2. Shift from does interaction work to which components work best for whom.
3. Corrective feedback is central with own meta-analytic tradition.
4. Computer-mediated interaction shows promise for L2 development.
5. Task characteristics influence interaction quantity/quality.

## Meta-analytic Evidence
- Keck (2006): 14 studies, large effects
- Mackey & Goo (2007): 28 studies, large effects especially delayed
- Lyster & Saito (2010): prompts more effective than recasts for some
- Li (2010): overall effectiveness confirmed
- Brown (2016): updated meta-analysis

## CMC Section (Critical for VibeLingo)
- Text-based CMC can support negotiation of meaning
- Slower text pace gives more processing time
- Written corrective feedback in CMC actively researched
- Consensus: interaction effective in technology-mediated contexts

## Implications for VibeLingo
1. Strong theoretical bedrock from interaction effectiveness.
2. CMC precedent confirms text modality is viable.
3. Embed interaction in tasks (vibe coding/research = natural task context).
4. Feedback spectrum: implicit to explicit show-me-why.
5. Delayed posttest effects: spaced review important.
6. Adapt feedback to learner individual differences.

## Limitations
- Predates LLM era (2018).
- CMC = human-to-human, not AI agents.
- Most studies on spoken interaction.

## Follow-up Questions
