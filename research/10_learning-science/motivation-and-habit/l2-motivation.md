# L2 Motivation: Self-Determination, Identity, and Sustained Language Learning

## Summary
Language learning motivation is best understood through two complementary frameworks: Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 1985, 2000) and the L2 Motivational Self System (Dornyei, 2005, 2009). SDT explains WHY learners persist (autonomy, competence, relatedness), while L2MSS explains what DRIVES them (visions of their future L2-using self). Together they provide a comprehensive lens for designing VibeLingo as a motivation-supporting rather than motivation-extracting tool.

## What We Know

### Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
- Three basic psychological needs predict sustained motivation: autonomy (feeling volitional), competence (feeling effective), relatedness (feeling connected).
- Motivation exists on a continuum from external regulation (rewards/punishment) to integrated regulation (the behaviour aligns with core identity and values).
- Extrinsic motivators (points, badges, streaks) can work short-term but typically fail to sustain behaviour, and can backfire via psychological reactance (Alberts et al., 2024).
- Autonomy is the most powerful and most neglected need in educational technology design.
- 53% of behaviour change apps are uninstalled within 30 days (Alberts et al., 2024).

### L2 Motivational Self System (L2MSS)
- Ideal L2 Self: the learner vision of themselves successfully using the target language. The strongest predictor of motivated behaviour.
- Ought-to L2 Self: external pressures and obligations (passing exams, meeting expectations). Weak predictor of sustained learning.
- L2 Learning Experience: the immediate quality of the learning environment. Directly shapes ongoing motivation.
- Meta-analysis confirms all three components significantly predict motivated behaviour (ResearchGate, 2018).

### Gamification: Mixed Evidence
- Gamification (points, leaderboards, streaks) can increase short-term engagement.
- However, it primarily drives extrinsic motivation, which wanes when novelty fades (Alberts et al., 2024).
- Psychological reactance: too-strong interventions cause behavioural backlash.
- Duolingo-style streaks may maintain app engagement but evidence for actual language proficiency gains is contested.

### Chatbot-Specific Motivation Findings
- Scoping review of 43 chatbot studies (Huang et al., 2025): chatbots generally correlated positively with motivation, engagement, and interest.
- But chatbots lagged behind human feedback and pedagogical approaches.
- Interactive/constructive chatbot designs were more motivating than passive designs.
- Only 4 longitudinal studies exist; most measured short-term motivation only.

## Design Relevance

### For VibeLingo
1. **The incidental learning framing directly serves integrated motivation.** The learner is not studying ¡ª they are working in their target language. This aligns with their Ideal L2 Self. No gamification needed.

2. **Autonomy must be the foundation.** Every learning intervention should be user-controlled. The agent suggests, never demands. Opt-in vocabulary capture, dismissible corrections, configurable intervention frequency.

3. **Competence through scaffolding, not testing.** In-the-moment help (translations, explanations, recasts) that makes the user feel more capable ¡ª not less.

4. **Relatedness through the agent itself.** The Synergy agent can serve as a consistent, warm, non-judgmental conversation partner. EDEN (Siyan et al., 2024) shows empathetic agent feedback correlates with learner grit.

5. **Contextual relevance is a key motivator.** LingoQ (Yang et al., 2026) showed that workers highly valued learning materials generated from their own work context. VibeLingo does this in real time.

6. **Avoid gamification unless proven necessary.** The core value proposition (I get to use my target language during real work) is intrinsically motivating. Adding points/streaks may undermine this by reframing the activity as a game rather than authentic communication.

## Risks / Misuses
- **Nudge fatigue**: Even well-intentioned autonomy-supportive nudges can feel intrusive if too frequent.
- **Learned helplessness**: Over-reliance on agent corrections can reduce learner independent problem-solving.
- **Fake empathy**: Agent emotional tone that feels inauthentic can damage trust and relatedness.
- **Privacy tension**: Emotion AI (Godwin-Jones, 2026) could detect disengagement but risks manipulation and profiling concerns.
- **Ought-to self trap**: If the user feels they SHOULD use the target language (ought-to) rather than wanting to, motivation will fade.

## Open Questions
- Does the incidental learning framing actually produce better outcomes than explicit study, or does it just feel better?
- How frequently should the agent intervene before it crosses from helpful to annoying?
- Can agent relatedness substitute for human interaction partners, or is it inherently weaker?
- What metrics should VibeLingo use to track motivation over months/years?
- How to detect when a user is losing motivation vs. just engaged in deep work?
