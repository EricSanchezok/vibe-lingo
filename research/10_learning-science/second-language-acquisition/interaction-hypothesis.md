# Interaction Hypothesis

## Summary
The Interaction Hypothesis (Long, 1981, 1996) states that second language acquisition is promoted when learners engage in meaningful communication and negotiate meaning during breakdowns. Key mechanisms: interaction makes input comprehensible through clarification requests, confirmation checks, and recasts; corrective feedback provides negative evidence; pushing learners to modify their output deepens acquisition. The 1996 revision incorporated a cognitive-interactionist perspective: interaction works by directing attention to linguistic forms during meaning-focused communication. Meta-analyses confirm large, durable effects.

## What We Know
- Robust meta-analytic support: multiple meta-analyses confirm large effect sizes, especially on delayed posttests.
- Interactionally modified input is superior to pre-simplified input.
- Negotiation strategies work: clarification requests, confirmation checks, and recasts are the core discourse moves.
- Corrective feedback is central: implicit recasts and explicit feedback both contribute.
- Computer-mediated interaction is viable: text-based CMC supports negotiation of meaning.
- Task design matters: most effective when embedded in tasks requiring genuine information exchange.
- Individual differences moderate effects: working memory, anxiety, and proficiency level influence outcomes.

## Design Relevance
### For VibeLingo core interaction model
The Interaction Hypothesis is the primary theoretical justification for VibeLingo: users interact with an agent in their target language, and the interaction itself drives acquisition. CRITICAL OPEN QUESTION: does human-LLM text interaction trigger the same mechanisms as human-human spoken interaction?

### Specific design implications
1. Negotiation architecture: the LLM should deliberately engineer communication breakdowns and use negotiation strategies.
2. Feedback spectrum: from implicit recasts during flow to explicit metalinguistic explanations via show-me-why.
3. Output push: the agent must require the user to produce language and reformulate.
4. Task embedding: language learning within real tasks (coding, research) rather than artificial exercises.
5. Cognitive load management: defer explicit feedback during high-cognitive-load tasks.
6. Spaced review: interaction benefits compound over time.

### Can human-LLM interaction be interaction?
- FOR: LLMs can produce all discourse moves. Text-based CMC is validated. AI may reduce affective filter.
- AGAINST: Learners may not negotiate meaning with AI the same way. Social-psychological aspects absent. No published research tests this directly.
- This gap is an opportunity for VibeLingo to contribute original research.

## Risks / Misuses
- Over-reliance on interaction (facilitative, not necessary).
- Forced negotiation becomes annoying and breaks trust.
- Neglecting individual differences.
- Assuming transfer from human-human theory without empirical validation.
