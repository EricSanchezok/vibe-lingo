# VibeLingo Product Context

## Register

Product software: calm, concise, and trustworthy. VibeLingo should feel like a thoughtful language coach embedded in a professional workspace, not a classroom exercise or developer console.

## Users

People who use Synergy for real coding, research, writing, and planning work while learning a target language. Their primary goal is completing the work; language learning should add value without interrupting it.

## Product Purpose

Help users learn a language from their own real-world expressions. Make authentic practice, useful corrections, recurring patterns, and review progress visible while preserving the user's agency and the flow of the main task.

## Brand Personality

Calm, encouraging, evidence-based, humane, and quietly optimistic. Feedback should feel specific and attainable rather than judgmental or gamified.

## Anti-references

- Blue-purple “AI” branding, gradients, glow, or decorative futurism.
- Debug dashboards, raw identifiers, confidence scores, or internal model states.
- Noisy nested cards, dense chrome, and navigation within navigation.
- Game mechanics, inflated scores, or claims of mastery unsupported by evidence.
- Language coaching that delays or takes over the user's real task.

## Design Principles

- Work first, coaching second.
- Show real evidence and activity; never manufacture progress.
- Use a restrained sage and warm amber palette for learning and attention states.
- Let typography, spacing, and hierarchy do most of the visual work.
- Keep surfaces flat, borders quiet, radii moderate, and shadows minimal.
- Prefer one clear next action and progressively disclose detail.
- Reuse Synergy's semantic theme tokens and behave naturally in light, dark, and custom themes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast expectations.
- Support keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen-reader labels, and reduced motion.
- Never use color as the only carrier of status.
- Handle long localized text and language names without clipping.
- Avoid shame, assumptions about proficiency, and claims that a user has permanently mastered a pattern.
